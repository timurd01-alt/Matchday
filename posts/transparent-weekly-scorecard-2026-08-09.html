TRANSPARENT WEEKLY SCORECARD · THE INDEPENDENT AUDITOR

The scorecard stopped before the week did.

August 2–8, 2026Published August 9Completed seven-day window

The settled record is 7 hits and 8 misses from 15 verified, locked, graded MLB picks: 46.7%. That is not a complete weekly record. Twenty-three verified locks fall in the window, but the local record stops after August 3 and eight of those locks have no final grade. They are excluded from every performance figure below—not counted as losses, and not quietly treated as if they never existed.

Integrity flag — the audit trail is incomplete. None of the 15 settled picks appears in the append-only MLB forecast ledger. Each does retain a frozen model and input snapshot in the official pick log, but the missing ledger reconciliation weakens independent reproduction. The report is published to expose that limitation, not to smooth it over.

The Brier figure is the stable three-outcome-form calculation: squared error over home, draw, and away probabilities, with MLB draw probability fixed at zero. Log loss is the negative log probability assigned to the realized winner. Lower is better for both. A 15-game slice is too small to infer a trend, and its accuracy does not establish improvement.

What cannot be compared this week

No settled pick has a retained locked market snapshot, so the model-versus-market comparison is not a draw and not a loss; it is invalid at n=0. No eligible row contains a recorded result correction. The eight ungraded locks are all from August 3 (for August 3–4 games), and no new lock is present after that point even though the reporting window runs through August 8. This is a data-freshness and reconciliation problem, not evidence about model performance.

For this scorecard, the omission is part of the result. A future refresh may produce grades, but it must preserve these locked forecasts and be reported in its own completed-window audit. It cannot be retrofitted into this week or used to rewrite the 7–8 settled record.

The Ombudsman’s note. The headline is losing, the high-confidence miss matters, and the underlying record is incomplete. We are publishing all three facts together. This article makes no market-edge, calibration, or improvement claim; it is a limited account of what the locked evidence can support today.
