METHOD NOTE · THE OMBUDSMAN

What we’re refining before we count more results

July 26, 2026System accountabilityNot a performance update

Good prediction reporting is not just a matter of putting a percentage beside a pick. It depends on being able to show what was known before a game, freeze that decision, and grade the same question after the game. We are tightening those seams before we let a larger record speak for us.

First, the constraint. The latest weekly scorecard had no eligible official picks. That is not a gap to paper over with older rows. It is the reason to improve the recordkeeping before making stronger claims.

The work is deliberately unglamorous

  1. Prove the pregame lock. Each official pick needs a stored lock time before kickoff, plus the probability and input snapshot that existed then. A result without that trail remains visible as unverified context, not scorekeeping evidence.
  2. Grade like for like. We are keeping the outcome basis explicit: a tournament advancement call is graded on advancement; a regulation market comparison is graded on regulation. The displayed score is not altered to make either conclusion fit.
  3. Keep the market comparison honest. Model-versus-market reporting needs both sides available for the same fixture and settlement basis. Missing market data is a missing comparison, not a reason to infer one.
  4. Make uncertainty visible. Accuracy alone does not establish improvement. Brier score, log loss, and calibration will be reported only when the locked sample supports them, with the cohort size beside every result.

Where MLB fits

MLB is back in Matchday’s active coverage and monitored list. Its fixture and result path is documented, and new MLB predictions can be evaluated under the same controls as every other sport. That does not retroactively qualify old rows: the historical MLB records reviewed this week do not have pregame lock timestamps, so they are not part of the official score.

This is a boundary, not a demotion. Baseball brings a large, frequent slate, which makes disciplined versioning more important—not less. MLB will enter the reported record only fixture by fixture once its lock evidence, outcome, and any market comparison can be reproduced.

What readers should expect next

The standard will stay stable: locked pregame picks only; stated sample sizes; misses given the same attention as hits; and no claim of progress from accuracy alone. If the sample is thin, the article will say so. If a result is corrected, the correction will be identified rather than silently replacing the earlier record.

Plainly put: refining the system is not an excuse for a blank week. It is the work required to make the next non-blank week worth reading.
