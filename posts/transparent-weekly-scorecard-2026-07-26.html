Metric definitions remain unchanged: multiclass Brier is the sum of squared error across home/draw/away probabilities (lower is better), and log loss is the negative log probability assigned to the realized outcome (lower is better). Neither answers a question with no verified observations. Calibration likewise requires comparing stated probabilities with outcomes in a nonzero cohort.

What was excluded, and why

The World Cup log contains one graded July 19 entry — Spain over Argentina, recorded 1–0 — but it lacks the pregame lock timestamp required by the current official-record rule. It remains visible as legacy/unverified context, not part of this score. The other public non-MLB logs contain no entries in the completed window.

MLB is also outside this article's official cohort. BALLDONTLIE is documented as an approved fixture/result provider, but the 66 graded MLB entries observed in this window have no pregame lock timestamp. Provider compliance does not substitute for lock evidence, so none is counted here. No market comparison is reported from excluded records.

Corrections and limitations

No correction was applied during this audit. The limitation is structural: the historical records in this window cannot prove that their predictions were frozen before kickoff. Until that evidence exists, accuracy, calibration, and market claims would be retrospective accounting, not an auditable scorecard.

The Ombudsman’s note. An empty ledger is disappointing because it offers no learning signal. It is still more accountable than borrowing legacy results to make a weekly record look complete.
