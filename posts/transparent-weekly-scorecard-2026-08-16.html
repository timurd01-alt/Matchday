A winning slice is not a clean bill of health.

August 9–15, 2026Published August 16Completed seven-day window

The official settled record is 26 hits and 20 misses from 46 verified, locked, graded MLB picks: 56.5%. That includes the misses. One further verified lock—Chicago Cubs over St. Louis on August 14—has no final grade and is excluded from every performance figure, not counted as a loss and not silently erased.

Integrity flag — the append-only ledger is still incomplete. All 47 window locks retain frozen prediction and input snapshots in the official pick log, but only 15 locks and 14 grades have reached the MLB forecast ledger. That is insufficient for a fully independent replay. The official score uses the verified receipts; the missing ledger coverage remains a reproducibility failure, not a reason to rewrite the record.

The Brier figure is the stable three-outcome-form score: squared error across home, draw, and away probabilities, with MLB draw probability fixed at zero. Log loss is the negative log probability assigned to the realized winner. Lower is better for both. A 46-game week is a sample, not a trend, and 56.5% accuracy alone does not show improvement.

The 60s band was close to its stated rate; the 50s band was not. The 70s band is three games, too small to call calibration. The best call was Tampa Bay at Oakland: a 73% away pick that won 8–4. The largest miss came on the same series a few days earlier, when Boston was a 72% home pick and lost 3–4. Those outcomes belong together: the good call does not cancel the miss.

Market comparison: usable, but not an edge claim

Each of the 46 settled picks has a regulation 1X2 market snapshot saved 11.64 to 16.02 seconds before its lock. On those same outcomes, the model went 26–20 (56.5%) and the market went 24–22 (52.2%). The model's Brier and log loss were lower by 0.006 and 0.005 respectively. That is a narrow, single-week comparison—not evidence of a persistent advantage.

There is a second limitation: the published model itself uses market context, so this is not a head-to-head test of an independent model against an external market. It is a like-for-like check that the retained model probability and the retained market probability were evaluated against the same final-winner outcome at the same pregame point. No market-edge or improvement claim follows from it.

What remains unresolved

The last stored lock is August 14, and no new lock appears on August 15 even though the reporting window continues through that date. The single August 14 Cubs–Cardinals lock is also ungraded. Neither absence is converted into a forecast result. A later grade must preserve the existing receipt and appear in a later completed-window report; it cannot be inserted here to revise the 26–20 score.

The Ombudsman’s note. This week is positive on the settled record, but it is not a victory lap. The high-confidence miss, the unresolved grade, the missing final day of locks, and the incomplete append-only ledger all limit what can responsibly be claimed. We are publishing the useful comparison alongside the failures that keep it provisional.
